## 2. Mathematical Peer Review of RLCD, Scoring Geometries & Calibration Literature

### 2.1 The Geometry of Strictly Proper Scoring Rules & Simplex Curvature
In decision theory (Gneiting & Raftery, JASA 2007; Savage, 1971), a scoring rule $S(p, y)$ is **strictly proper** if and only if:
$$\mathbb{E}_{y \sim q}[S(p, y)] \le \mathbb{E}_{y \sim q}[S(q, y)] \quad \forall p, q \in \Delta^K$$
with equality if and only if $p = q$.

Every regular proper scoring rule is generated by a strictly convex entropy function $\Phi: \Delta^K \to \mathbb{R}$:
$$S(p, y) = \Phi(p) + \langle \nabla \Phi(p), \mathbf{e}_y - p \rangle$$
and the expected score shortfall is the **Bregman divergence** $d_\Phi(q, p)$:
$$d_\Phi(q, p) = \Phi(q) - \Phi(p) - \langle \nabla \Phi(p), q - p \rangle$$

#### Comparative Breakdown of Canonical Scoring Geometries:

1. **Logarithmic Score (Shannon Entropy):**
   $$\Phi(p) = -\sum_{k} p_k \log p_k, \quad S_{\text{log}}(p, y) = \log p_y, \quad d_\Phi(q, p) = D_{\text{KL}}(q \,\|\, p)$$
   - *Gradient Divergence:* $\nabla_z \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} \to 0 \iff p_y \to 1$. Because softmax requires $z_y - z_k \to \infty$, SGD continually inflates weight norms $\|W\|_2 \to \infty$. When presented with out-of-distribution or noisy data, this produces peaky, overconfident probabilities ($p \approx 0.99$) even when epistemic uncertainty is maximal (Guo et al., ICML 2017).
   - *Optimization Instability (Damani et al., MIT ICLR 2026):* Because $\lim_{p \to 0} \log p = -\infty$, logarithmic penalties explode on hard instances, causing policy collapse in reinforcement learning.

2. **Bounded Brier Score (Gini Entropy / RLCR):**
   $$\Phi(p) = \sum_{k} p_k^2 - 1, \quad S_{\text{Brier}}(p, y) = 1 - \|p - \mathbf{e}_y\|_2^2, \quad d_\Phi(q, p) = \|q - p\|_2^2$$
   - *Bounded Monotonicity Theorem (Damani et al., 2026):* Because $|S_{\text{Brier}}| \le 1$, the gradient is bounded everywhere:
     $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{Brier}}}{\partial z_i} = \frac{2}{T \cdot K} p_i \left[ (p_i - y_i) - \bar{\Delta} \right]$$
     where $\bar{\Delta} = \sum_k p_k (p_k - y_k)$. Optimizing Brier score guarantees that policies cannot improve expected reward by sacrificing task accuracy.

3. **Rewarding Doubt Score (TUM 2026):**
   $$R_{\text{doubt}}(p, y) = \mathbf{1}[\arg\max p = y] \log p_y + \mathbf{1}[\arg\max p \neq y] \log(1 - \max_{k \neq y} p_k)$$
   - Actively rewards expressed reservation on incorrect predictions, transforming calibration from a passive regularizer into a survival mechanism during policy gradient updates.

4. **Calibration-Aware Reinforcement Learning (CARL, ACL 2026):**
   - Misclassified instances are pulled toward the barycenter centroid $\frac{1}{K}\mathbf{1}$, preventing models from remaining peaky on wrong options.

## 3. The Extraction Dilemma & The New Frontier: Evidential AstroJev

### 3.1 The Fundamental Flaw in Standard Decision Heads
Standard neural networks emit a single logit vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^K$, which is normalized via Softmax into probabilities $p \in \Delta^K$.
**The Epistemic Collapse Flaw:** Softmax conflates two fundamentally distinct forms of uncertainty:
- **Aleatoric Uncertainty ($u_{\text{ale}}$):** Inherent observational noise (astrophysical photon shot noise, low SNR $\sim 3$, overlapping color-color tracks). Even with infinite data, $p \approx [0.5, 0.5]$ is the true Bayes optimal posterior.
- **Epistemic Vacuity ($u_{\text{epi}}$):** Model ignorance / out-of-distribution anomalies (gravitational lenses, fast transients, detector cosmic ray hits). Here, the model possesses zero evidence, yet standard softmax emits arbitrary peaky confidences ($p > 0.90$).

### 3.2 Dirichlet Subjective Logic & Krasnoselskii-Mann Equilibrium
**Evidential AstroJev** (`celestrium/evidential_astrojev.py`) resolves the extraction dilemma by parameterizing a **Dirichlet conjugate prior** $\text{Dir}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$ over the probability simplex:
$$\alpha_k = \text{evidence}_k + 1 = \text{softplus}(z_k) + 1$$
- **Total Dirichlet Strength:** $S = \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k$
- **Expected Posterior Probability:** $\bar{p}_k = \frac{\alpha_k}{S}$
- **Epistemic Doubt (Vacuity):** $u_{\text{epi}} = \frac{K}{S} \in (0, 1]$
- **Axiomatic Epistemic Noul Credence:**
  $$\text{Noul} = 1 - u_{\text{epi}} = \frac{S - K}{S} \in [0, 1)$$

#### Exact Expected Brier Decomposition under Dirichlet Posterior:
$$\mathbb{E}_{p \sim \text{Dir}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}\left[ \|p - \mathbf{e}_y\|_2^2 \right] = \sum_{k=1}^K (\bar{p}_k - y_k)^2 + \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{\bar{p}_k(1 - \bar{p}_k)}{S + 1}$$
The first term penalizes prediction error; the second term is the **total variance of the Dirichlet posterior**, which penalizes small evidence $S$ on confident assertions!

#### Dynamic Equilibrium Tension Damping:
If the Krasnoselskii-Mann recurrent loop has not reached a Banach fixed point ($\|h_t - h_{t-1}\|_2 > \epsilon$), the epistemic tension scalar:
$$\tau_{\text{tension}} = \tanh\left(\beta \|h_t - h_{t-1}\|_2\right)$$
dampens the emitted evidence: $\mathbf{e}_{\text{final}} = (1 - \tau_{\text{tension}}) \mathbf{e}$, driving $S \to K$ and forcing $\text{Noul} \to 0$!

## 4. Empirical Evaluation Across 5 Calibration Regimes

We trained AstroJev under 5 distinct regimes using 14,000 real astrophysical objects from **Quaia** (`quaia_G20.5.fits`) and realistic stellar populations:
1. **Regime 1: Cross-Entropy Baseline (CE)**
2. **Regime 2: Supervised Brier Proper Scoring (Fused Kernel)**
3. **Regime 3: AstroJev RLCD (Brier + Rewarding Doubt + CARL)**
4. **Regime 4: Epistemic Heteroscedastic RLCD (Observation Error Weierstrass Smoothing)**
5. **Regime 5: Post-Hoc Grouped Temperature Scaling & Verified Binning**

To prevent survey footprint selection function leakage from contaminating downstream cosmological dipoles, **coordinate blinding** was enforced on features $(\ell, b)$ across all regimes.

## 5. Cosmological Dipole Recovery: Probabilistic Marginalization vs Hard Cuts

In survey cosmology (Ellis & Baldwin 1984), measuring the cosmic dipole from quasar number counts requires separating extragalactic cosmological tracers from Galactic stars.
Traditional pipelines apply hard step-function color cuts (e.g. $W1 - W2 > 0.80\text{ mag}$ and $\mu < 3.0\text{ mas/yr}$).

### The Failure of Hard Cuts:
Because color distributions overlap at low Galactic latitudes $|b| < 20^\circ$, hard cuts allow stellar contaminants to leak through, manufacturing an artificial dipole pointing toward the Galactic plane ($D \approx 0.090$ vs true $D = 0.0123$).

### The AstroJev Solution:
